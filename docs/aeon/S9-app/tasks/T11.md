## Why this exists

A slice is not done when its tests pass. It is done when someone who did not build it can follow `DEMO.md` and watch
the thing work (§14).

**And this is the slice where the CLI stops being the only way in.** D21: nothing in the demo may require a terminal,
except the deliberate `pkill` and `make dev`. A demo step that reaches for a shell is the finding, not a workaround.

## Scope

### Walk every section

End to end on a throwaway repository, fixing whatever does not match — the demo or the code, whichever is wrong.

**§4 is the section that matters most, and it has three parts for a reason.**

- **Part one:** `pkill -9` the daemon while a loop runs. The UI must go **stale with an age, a cause and the socket
  path** — not stay confident. A UI showing `running` when it has not confirmed anything for forty seconds is the most
  believable lie in the product.
- **Part two:** let a loop sit at a **gate for five minutes** and touch nothing. It must stay **`current`** — no
  warning, no age, no badge. **Silence is not staleness.** A UI that cries wolf makes part one's warning worthless, so
  this part failing invalidates the other two.
- **Part three:** restart the daemon. The app must **re-read** and arrive at true current state, including whatever
  happened while it was disconnected — not resume from its last `seq`.

**§5 is the thesis.** An `unknown` cost must render its reason; `partial` must read as **"at least $X"** in words. If
an unknown renders as `$0.00`, four slices of design work were wasted at the last mile, and this is the only place
that gets caught. Check the **total** too: a total over a `partial` step must itself read as a floor.

**§2's identical questions.** A step question and a gate, answered from one thread with no indication which is which.
Then double-submit one and confirm nothing renders until the daemon confirms.

**§8 is the invariant that looks like paranoia.** `lsof` the app's processes and confirm **no store file is open**. It
is what keeps the store from becoming an accidental public contract.

### Verify the QA amendment

`docs/test_suites/aeon-ui.md` holds **QAU-01..08** from v1, and **the S9 amendment is already written**: a
*"What changed underneath this suite"* note, a disposition for every existing case, and **QAU-09..18**. This task's job
is to **verify it against the built app and correct it**, not to author it.

Read that section first. Three claims in it are the ones to check hardest, because each is a correction rather than an
addition:

- **QAU-06's original expectation was wrong**, not merely incomplete. It required the stream to "reconcile by `seq`",
  which U13/D7 forbids: a **filtered** subscription legitimately sees gaps (S4 made `seq` the `event` rowid), so a gap
  tells the app nothing. The amended case demands a **re-read** and marks `seq` reconciliation a FAIL. Confirm the built
  app does the former.
- **Three framing assumptions are superseded**: U1 is gone (the Tauri shell is the UI, not a served origin), the stub is
  a stubbed **bridge** rather than a server, and there is no HTTP in the app at all. A tester reading the old cases
  without that note will waste a morning.
- **QAU-04's state names changed.** S8 produces `running`/`waiting`/`blocked`/`done`; `stalled` and `failed` were v1's
  vocabulary. Its requirement stands and sharpens: `waiting` must not share styling with `blocked`.

QAU-01 (permissions, S11) and QAU-05 (diffs, S16) are marked **not yet applicable with their slice named, never
deleted** — a checklist that shrinks when a feature is missing is a checklist that forgets. QAU-05's reasoning about
silent caps is inherited by QAU-17.
