## Why this exists

## Scope

Five rewrites, T4's pattern, plus the following.

### `docker-engineer` owns the corpus's two mid-pattern wildcards

Its policy contains `docker buildx build*--push*` and `docker buildx bake*--push*` — **the only two patterns in the
entire 366-rule corpus with a `*` in the middle**, and the reason S11's matcher cannot be prefix-only (G9).

Its policy must be **byte-identical to S11's extractor output** (A16, D5), and this is the handler where a
re-parse would be caught: any normalisation that moved or dropped a `*` changes what the guard permits.

Its domain — base-image strategy, multi-stage builds, what belongs in a layer — survives. Its instruction not to push
is now **policy**, not prose (A12's middle category).

### `security-engineer` and `legal-advisor` are advisory, and must stay so

Both originals frame their output as **advice, not authority**: a legal review is not a lawyer's opinion, a security
assessment is not a penetration test or a clearance.

**That framing is domain content and it must survive the rewrite.** It is also exactly the kind of line A4 warned about
— it reads like a disclaimer, which reads like boilerplate, which is the first thing a shortening pass cuts. Losing it
turns a handler that offers analysis into one that appears to certify, and a loop consuming that output would treat a
gap-analysis as a sign-off.

Assert it: both bodies must retain their advisory framing, and a body that reads as authoritative fails.

### `security-engineer` and the nine tools

Its original assumes tooling Aeon does not have. Whatever it says about scanners, SAST or dependency audits that
require an absent capability is `DEFERRED` (A18) — recorded with the capability named, not dropped. What survives is the
**judgement**: what to look for, what a finding's severity means, what is worth blocking on.

### `terraform-engineer` and irreversibility

Its domain includes when a plan is destructive and when state is at risk. That is judgement of exactly the kind S11's
`ask` verdict exists for — but **there is no `terraform` pattern in the corpus**, so nothing is denied or asked by
policy here.

**Do not add policy in this task.** If this handler warrants an `ask` rule, that is a finding to raise: policy is data in
an agent definition, S11's extractor reads it from `opencode/`, and `opencode/` is read-only to this slice (A10, D14).

### `ui-design-engineer`

Its domain is visual and interaction judgement, and its output is a specification rather than code. Like T7's handlers,
what goes is any instruction about where to put the file.
