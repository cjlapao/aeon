## Why this exists

The schedule is where TR1 is enforced — and TR1 is what keeps this slice eight tasks instead of twenty.

> **A schedule fires a named blueprint. It never selects work.**

Work selection needs a roadmap, a definition of *"ready"*, queueing and caps. **All of those are S27's**,
and the existing Stage 5 sketch conflates them with the clock. **If a field here accepts a query, a filter
or a readiness rule, this slice has absorbed S27** and will not land.

**And the timezone is explicit** (TR6). Not an offset — an offset does not know about DST. Not the host's
local zone — that changes when somebody moves a container, silently, in production.

**Read first:** the design's TR1, TR6, TR8; S8's blueprint pinning (L6) — a schedule names a blueprint, and
the pinning is the loop's; `robfig/cron/v3`'s expression support.

## Scope

**1. The schedule**: a **named blueprint**, an expression, an **IANA zone**, an optional working-hours
window, and an id.

**2. No work selection.** Assert it: no field accepts a query, a filter, a tag, a priority or a readiness
predicate. **This is a test, because it is a boundary somebody will erode with one convenient field.**

**3. A missing zone is refused**, not defaulted. The refusal names the problem and suggests the IANA form,
because the natural thing to type is `BST` or `+01:00` and neither is a zone.

**4. Answer Q1: the expression syntax.** **A subset is defensible** — `@daily`, `@hourly` and a five-field
expression cover the stated need, and every syntax feature is one more thing to validate, explain and get
wrong. **Decide with the demo in front of you**, and state what is deliberately excluded.

**5. An unparseable expression is refused at write time**, not at the first tick. A schedule that stores
cleanly and then never fires is the worst failure available here: nothing is wrong, nothing happens.

**6. The window is optional** (TR8). Absent, it runs whenever it fires — because *"the agents worked all
weekend"* is a feature to some people and a surprise bill to others, which is a per-deployment judgement
rather than a default.

**7. The migration follows S4's discipline** exactly, and fresh-equals-migrated still holds.
