# T8 — `control.go`, and narrowing S27's sketch

## Why this exists

**Two off switches that must not be confused** (TR12):

| | | Lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`TriggerAccess`** | *Governance* — "no webhooks in this deployment" | policy, operator-authored |
| **pause / resume** | *Operational control* — "not right now" | **CLI and app only** |

**Both stop a loop firing. They are different facts.** Pause is a decision somebody may undo this
afternoon; the policy is a deployment-wide rule with an author, a record and a lifetime.

**And pause is not on the narrow surface.** R6's reasoning: operational control there would hand a caller
the means to bypass the guards written to constrain it. **A scheduler needs an off switch that is not
"uninstall"** — and that switch is local.

**The second half of this task is housekeeping that matters.** `S27-continuous/SKETCH.md` currently
conflates the clock with work selection. **This slice built the clock**, so the sketch must be narrowed to
selection only — otherwise S27 gets authored against a document that claims work this slice already did.

**Read first:** R6 and N14 in [`../../S19-narrow-surface/design.md`](../../S19-narrow-surface/design.md);
[`../../S27-continuous/SKETCH.md`](../../S27-continuous/SKETCH.md) as it stands; the design's TR12.

## Scope

**1. `aeon trigger list | pause | resume | fire-now`.**

`fire-now` is a convenience and it must be honest: it fires **as a trigger**, so the loop is still
**unattended**. **Do not let it fire as the invoking user** — that would be an attendedness override
through the back door, which is exactly what TR2 forbids.

**2. Pause is distinct from `TriggerAccess`.** Both reachable, both reported, never conflated in output.
`trigger list` shows `PAUSED`; a policy refusal shows S10's sentence.

**2a. `trigger.list` / `trigger.pause` / `trigger.resume` / `trigger.fireNow` are registered socket
methods (D-equivalent, I10, P34 — added 2026-10-26).** The table above already says pause/resume live in
"CLI and app" — this is where that becomes real rather than aspirational. Each socket method calls the
identical `internal/trigger/control.go` function its CLI verb calls; not a second control-plane
implementation. `fire-now`'s honesty rule (it fires as a trigger, unattended, never as the invoking caller)
applies identically over the socket — a person clicking "fire now" in the app must see the same unattended
loop a CLI `fire-now` produces, never a quiet attended exception just because a human was watching.

**3. Nothing here on the narrow surface.** Assert by grep over S19's action table: no pause, resume,
fire-now, or their socket-method names.

**4. Narrow `S27-continuous/SKETCH.md`.** Remove what this slice delivered — the clock, the firing, the
unattendedness reasoning — and **keep what is genuinely S27's**: work selection, *"ready"*, queueing,
caps, and the roadmap dependency.

**Record that the clock is S20's**, with a pointer, so the next reader does not re-derive it. **Preserve
the sketch's open questions** that still apply; they were written for a reason.

## Notes (what this task actually found, in its own words)

**One control plane, proved at the call sites, not by trust.** `aeon trigger
list|pause|resume|fire-now|history` dial the five socket methods; the daemon's
seam (`trigger_control.go`) calls `d.triggers.List/Pause/Resume/FireNow` and
nothing else; the CLI imports neither store nor trigger.
`TestTriggerSocketAndCLICallSameControlFunctions` reads all three files'
SOURCE and fails if any leg grows its own logic (P34). It strips comments
before scanning — measured the hard way, when my own honest prose about
attendedness tripped the scan: a source-scan that punishes explanation
selects for files that stop explaining themselves, which is the wrong
incentive.

**fire-now earned two rulings while being built.** (i) **A webhook row refuses
fire-now** — refused and RECORDED: the signature is the delivery's entire
authentication, so a convenience that could start a webhook's loop unsigned
is the bypass wearing a friendly face. (ii) **A paused trigger answers
fire-now `did_not_fire` with the TR12 sentence, and records it** — Q3's
silence governs the AUTOMATIC paths (a paused schedule owes the clock no
answers); a human pressing a button deserves an answer in the history, and
the outcome word stays operational (`did_not_fire`, never `refused`, which
is governance's word — `TestPauseAndTriggerAccessAreDistinct` pins that the
two planes never borrow each other's vocabulary, listing included: no policy
word may appear in the listing, because a plane that cannot evaluate
governance must not print its implicit consent).

**The `manual` kind is vocabulary, not a row — and the schema says so.** The
policy closed-three (manual|schedule|webhook) is complete at the governance
layer, but migration 0032's CHECK admits only schedule and webhook, and
migrations are immutable. No manual row ships without a migration; none was
invented to make a test read nicer. `fireAt` now asks governance for
`string(s.Kind)`, so when a manual producer arrives (with its migration), it
will be governed as what it is. Named, not hidden.

**The forbidden-word guard had a blind spot, measured before fixed:** the
tokenizer split on `_ . -` only, so `trigger.fireNow` tokenized as
["trigger","firenow"] — and "fire" in `ForbiddenOperationalControl` would
have matched NOTHING. camelCase is now a word boundary (the poison entry
`aeon_trigger_fire_now → trigger.fireNow` fails with "token fire"; the
existing scans over paths and names are unaffected — measured, they speak
lowercase). A guard that reads one token where two words stand is present,
green, and decorative.

**S27's sketch stands narrowed.** The clock, the firing and the unattendedness
reasoning are recorded as S20's — BUILT — with the pointer (STATUS's S20 row
is the receipt); what survives as genuinely S27's is work selection, "ready",
queueing, caps, and the blocked-item question. Its open questions survived on
purpose, two of them sharpened by reality: the working-hours window is SOLVED
for schedules (TR8) and still open for the finish-triggered start (what does
"did not fire" even mean when the answer came from a finish, not a tick?);
the off-switch question now has a shipped pattern (`trigger pause|resume`,
narrow-forbidden) and a new question of its own — does pausing the trigger
stop the continuous worker, or does S27 need its own switch shaped the same
way? `TestS27SketchIsSelectionOnly` fails if the pre-S20 framing returns.
