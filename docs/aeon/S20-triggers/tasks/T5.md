## Why this exists

**This is a task of its own because it is the easiest thing in the slice to defer forever.**

Two days a year, in one timezone at a time, in a component nobody watches. As two checklist lines inside
another task they get ticked; as a task they get done.

**Spring forward** — the wall-clock time does not exist. `02:30` on the last Sunday in March in
`Europe/London` never happens. A naive scheduler either fires at an arbitrary nearby instant or **does not
fire and does not say so.**

**Autumn back** — the wall-clock time happens twice. `01:30` occurs at `01:30 BST` and again at
`01:30 GMT`. A naive scheduler **fires twice**, which for a job that posts something is a duplicate nobody
can explain.

**Decisions:** non-existent is **skipped and recorded**; doubled **fires once** (TR6).

**Read first:** `clock.go` (T2); Go's `time.LoadLocation` and how `time.Date` resolves ambiguous and
non-existent wall-clock times — **it does not error, it picks one**, which is why this needs explicit
handling.

## Scope

**1. A non-existent wall-clock time is skipped**, and the skip is **recorded with its reason** — not as a
generic `did not fire`. *"That time does not exist in this zone"* is the only useful message here.

**2. A doubled wall-clock time fires once.** The record notes that the second occurrence was skipped as
already-fired, so the duplicate's absence is explained rather than inferred.

**3. The tests use a real IANA zone**, not a fixed offset. **A fixed offset has no DST**, so a test written
with one passes while testing nothing — and it will look correct in review. **Assert that a real zone is
loaded.**

**4. Pick zones with opposite transitions** if practical — a northern and a southern hemisphere zone — so
the tests do not encode an assumption that clocks go forward in March.

**5. State in the notes how Go resolved each case** before you handled it. The next person will hit this
and the behaviour is not obvious from the documentation.
