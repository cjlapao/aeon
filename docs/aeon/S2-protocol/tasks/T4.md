# T4 — Events: kinds, `seq`, the cursor, lifecycle emission

**File(s):** `aeon/internal/protocol/events.go`, `aeon/internal/daemon/lifecycle.go`, `aeon/internal/protocol/events_test.go`
**Engineer:** golang-engineer-loop · **Tech:** go · **Depends on:** T3 · **Est. changed lines:** ~280

> ## Notes — done 2026-10-15
>
> ### The S4 change that replaces the in-memory counter
>
> **The store's `event` table becomes the allocator and the backing for replay.** `seq` becomes a column
> with an index, monotonic across restarts, and `EventLog` becomes a cache in front of it rather than the
> source of truth. S4's schema task owns the migration. Until then the limitation is stated in a doc comment
> **on the counter itself** — where somebody about to depend on it is reading — and in the demo, and in
> `conformance/README.md`.
>
> ### One deviation from N6, recorded rather than resolved silently
>
> **N6 says "no server-side replay buffer". T4's validation requires `after: n` to replay the tail across
> twenty events at three cursor positions. Both cannot be literally true** — serving a cursor into the past
> means having the past.
>
> What is built: **one bounded ring (1024, a guess), shared by every subscription, that forgets.** It holds
> no client-specific state and cannot grow with the number of clients, which is the leak N6 and T5 are
> actually about. When a cursor is older than the ring, the subscription resumes at the oldest retained seq
> and **`from` reports it** — so the gap is named, not hidden. That is the difference between a bounded ring
> and a buffer pretending to be a log.
>
> The reasoning is in `events.go`'s doc comment, with a test asserting it stays there, so the next person to
> read N6 finds out why it is not literal.
>
> ### Concurrency, where "monotonic" quietly becomes false
>
> `seq` is allocated and the ring appended **under one lock**. Allocating outside it would let two emitters
> write the ring out of order, so the counter would be monotonic and what a subscriber receives would not. A
> 200-goroutine test asserts both the counter and the ring's order.
>
> ### Three findings from the live walk
>
> A watcher with no filter **also receives `subscription.created` for its own subscription** — correct, and
> unpredicted. The replay is delivered **before** `subscription.created`, so a client cannot mistake its
> backlog for live traffic. And `client.connected` carries `peerPidAvailable`, because a bare `0` cannot
> distinguish "pid is zero" from "no pid on this platform" and inventing one sends somebody to kill the
> wrong process.

## Context

Separate from T3 because ordering and filtering fail differently, and because this task carries the one thing
that makes subscriptions testable at all in a slice with no domain.

**The daemon emits its own lifecycle events** (T8). There are no loops, steps or stored events yet, so a
subscriber would have nothing to receive — and subscriptions written blind now would first be exercised at S5,
four slices later, with the loop engine as a confounding variable. A client connecting is a real event. `aeon
watch` in one terminal while another runs `aeon ping` exercises filtering, delivery and sequencing for real.

**`seq` is in-memory in this slice, and says so** (O2). The store's `event` table is S4, and it is what makes
the cursor survive a restart. An in-memory counter that *looks* durable is worse than one that admits it —
somebody will build a client that trusts it across a daemon restart. So the doc comment states the limitation
and names the S4 change that removes it, and the demo says the same.

**The cursor is asserted over a run, not an event** (D5). "Monotonic" is trivially true of one event. What
matters is no gaps and no duplicates across a sequence, including across a subscribe-with-`after`.

**Read first:** `subscribe.go` (T3); the design's §Subscriptions; `docs/_archive/aeon-v1-horizontal/02-store/design.md` for
the `event` table `seq` will move onto at S4.

## What to do

**1. `events.go`** — the event envelope: `subscriptionId`, `seq`, `kind`, `projectRoot`, `at`, and a
kind-specific payload. Notifications are JSON-RPC `method: "event"`.

The four kinds this slice emits: `daemon.started`, `client.connected`, `client.disconnected`,
`subscription.created`.

**2. `seq`** — monotonic within a stream, allocated once per event, never reused. **Document that it is
in-memory and resets on restart** (O2), and name the S4 change that replaces it. Put that in a doc comment on
the counter itself, where someone about to depend on it will read it.

**3. The cursor** — `subscribe {after: n}` replays from `n+1` and returns `from` so the client knows where it
actually resumed. `after` beyond the current head is not an error: it means "nothing yet", and returns
`from` = head+1.

**4. `lifecycle.go`** — emit the four kinds from the accept path and the registry. `client.connected` carries
the client name, version and pid where available (S1's peer credentials, D12).

**5. Test the cursor over a sequence.** Emit twenty events, subscribe with `after` at three different points,
and assert each delivery is exactly the tail from that point — no gap, no duplicate, no reordering.
